How to Calculate GPA: Step-by-Step Guide With Examples + Free Calculator

By ChrisJuly 9, 2026 · 8 min readAcademics

You just got your grades back. An A in psych, a B+ in biology, and a C in calculus. Your GPA this semester is... what exactly? And more importantly — what does it mean for your cumulative GPA, your scholarship, and that internship you're applying for?

GPA looks confusing at first because it involves letters, numbers, and credit hours all at once. But the formula behind it is dead simple. Once you understand it, you can calculate any GPA situation — semester, cumulative, weighted, or what-if scenarios.

This guide walks through every type of GPA calculation with real numbers, explains why the most common mistake people make (averaging semester GPAs) is wrong, and includes a free calculator so you never have to do the math by hand again.

1. The Core Formula: GPA = Quality Points ÷ Credits

Every GPA on earth uses the same basic formula. Once you get this, everything else is just variations.

The Formula

GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit Hours Attempted

Where Quality Points for one course = (Credit Hours) × (Grade Point Value).

The 4.0 Scale (Unweighted)

Every letter grade maps to a number. This is the standard scale used by almost all US colleges and most high schools:
LetterGrade PointsLetterGrade Points
A4.0C+2.3
A-3.7C2.0
B+3.3C-1.7
B3.0D+1.3
B-2.7D1.0
F0.0
Some schools use A+ as 4.3 and D- as 0.7. Check your school's specific scale — variations exist. But 95% of US institutions use the table above.

2. Semester GPA: Step-by-Step (Unweighted)

This is the simplest case — one semester, standard scale, no frills.

The Steps

Here's exactly what you do:
Step 1: List every course, its credit hours, and your letter grade.
Step 2: Convert each letter grade to its grade point value.
Step 3: Multiply credit hours × grade points to get quality points per course.
Step 4: Add up all quality points.
Step 5: Add up all credit hours.
Step 6: Divide total quality points by total credit hours.

Example: A Real Semester

Say this is your schedule:

Semester GPA = 43.0 ÷ 12 = 3.58

That's it. That's your GPA for this semester. Feels good, right? But what if you want to know how this semester changes your overall GPA? That's where cumulative comes in.

3. Cumulative GPA: Why You Can't Just Average Semesters

This is the most common mistake students make — and it can throw your GPA off by a lot.

The Wrong Way

You might be tempted to add your semester GPAs and divide by the number of semesters. Say semester 1 was 3.58 and semester 2 was 3.00. Average = (3.58 + 3.00) ÷ 2 = 3.29. Seems logical. But it's wrong if your semesters had different credit loads.

The Right Way

Cumulative GPA = (Total Quality Points from ALL semesters) ÷ (Total Credits from ALL semesters). You have to add up every single quality point and every single credit across your entire academic history, then divide.

Example: Two Semesters

Semester 1 (from above): 12 credits, 43.0 quality points, GPA = 3.58

Semester 2:
CourseCreditsGradeGrade PointsQuality Points
Psychology3B3.09.0
History3C+2.36.9
Art3A4.012.0
Total927.9
Correct cumulative calculation:
Total quality points = 43.0 + 27.9 = 70.9
Total credits = 12 + 9 = 21
Cumulative GPA = 70.9 ÷ 21 = 3.38
Wrong way (averaging semesters): (3.58 + 3.10) ÷ 2 = 3.34. Off by 0.04 in this case, but the gap gets bigger when credit loads differ more.
Remember: you can only average semester GPAs directly if every semester had exactly the same number of credits. Otherwise, you're doing the math wrong.

4. Weighted GPA: The 5.0 Scale

Weighted GPA applies mostly to high school students taking AP, IB, honors, or dual-credit courses. Some colleges also use it.

How Weighting Works

Instead of the standard 4.0 for an A, weighted scales add bonus points for harder courses. The most common system:

Regular courses: A = 4.0 (standard)
Honors/pre-AP: A = 4.5 (+0.5)
AP/IB/dual credit: A = 5.0 (+1.0)

The bonus applies to all letter grades, not just A's. So a B in an AP class would be 4.0 (3.0 + 1.0), not 3.0.

Example: Weighted GPA

Same grade but with AP and honors courses:

Same schedule without weighting would be 3.86. The AP class pushed it above 4.0.
Weighted GPA policies vary wildly by school district. Some cap the bonus at certain levels, others only weight certain subjects. Check your school's policy before assuming.

5. Goal Planning: What GPA You Need Next Semester

This is where GPA math becomes useful — figuring out what you need to hit a target.

The Formula

Required GPA = (Target Cumulative × Total Future Credits — Current Quality Points) ÷ Future Credits

Looks complicated, but it's just algebra. Here's how it works with real numbers.

Example: You Want a 3.5

You've completed 30 credits with a 3.2 GPA. You have 15 credits left this year. What GPA do you need in those 15 credits to reach a 3.5 cumulative?
Step 1: Current quality points = 3.2 × 30 = 96.0
Step 2: Target quality points = 3.5 × (30 + 15) = 3.5 × 45 = 157.5
Step 3: Quality points needed = 157.5 — 96.0 = 61.5
Step 4: Required GPA = 61.5 ÷ 15 = 4.1

A 4.1 is tough but doable if you load up on easier courses. If the number comes out above 4.0, your goal isn't realistic with the credits you have left — you'd need to retake some classes or extend your timeline.

6. Retakes, Pass/Fail & Special Cases

Not every course counts the same way. Here's what to know.

Retaking a Course

Most US colleges use one of two policies:

Grade replacement: The new grade replaces the old one in your GPA calculation. The old grade usually stays on your transcript but doesn't factor into GPA. This is common at public universities.

Grade averaging: Both grades are averaged together, so retaking doesn't fully erase a bad grade. This is more common at private schools.

Check your school's policy before assuming retaking will fix your GPA.

Pass/Fail Courses

Courses taken pass/fail are usually excluded from GPA calculation if you pass. If you fail, some schools count the F as 0.0 while others exclude it. CR (credit) and P (pass) typically don't affect GPA. NC (no credit) and F might.

Withdrawals & Incompletes

W (withdrawal): Usually not counted in GPA, but too many W's can affect financial aid.
WP/WF: WP (withdrew passing) doesn't affect GPA. WF (withdrew failing) may count as an F at some schools.
I (incomplete): Not counted until the grade is submitted. If it stays incomplete too long, some schools convert it to an F.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my GPA on a 4.0 scale?
Multiply each course's credit hours by its grade points (A=4.0, B+=3.3, etc.), add up all quality points, then divide by total credit hours. Example: a 3-credit A (12 points) + a 4-credit B (12 points) = 24 points ÷ 7 credits = 3.43 GPA.
What's the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?
Unweighted GPA uses a standard 4.0 scale. Weighted adds bonus points for advanced courses — typically +0.5 for honors and +1.0 for AP/IB/dual credit. An A in AP equals 5.0 instead of 4.0, so weighted GPAs can exceed 4.0.
How do I calculate my cumulative GPA from multiple semesters?
Add up ALL quality points from ALL semesters, then divide by ALL credit hours. Do NOT average your semester GPAs — that only works if every semester had the same number of credits. Correct formula: (QP1 + QP2 + ...) ÷ (Credits1 + Credits2 + ...).
What GPA do I need to stay off academic probation?
Most US colleges require a minimum 2.0 cumulative GPA to stay in good standing. Scholarships typically require 3.0+. Honors programs require 3.5+. Dean's list is usually 3.5-3.7.
Does retaking a course replace the old grade?
Most schools use either grade replacement (new grade replaces old in GPA) or grade averaging (both grades count). Check your school's specific policy — some also cap the replacement benefit.
What grades are excluded from GPA?
Passed pass/fail courses (CR/P), withdrawals (W), and incompletes (I) are typically excluded. Failed pass/fail courses may count as 0.0 depending on your school.

Quick Recap

  • Core formula: GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credits
  • Semester GPA: Credits × grade points, sum, divide. Takes 2 minutes.
  • Cumulative GPA: Combine ALL quality points and ALL credits across semesters. Don't average semester GPAs.
  • Weighted GPA: Advanced courses get +0.5 (honors) or +1.0 (AP/IB) per grade.
  • Goal planning: Target GPA × total credits — current QP = what you need next.
  • Retakes: Replacement vs. averaging — know your school's policy.
