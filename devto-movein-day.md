# What College Move-In Day Is Really Like (And How to Survive It)

I showed up to my dorm freshman year with a car packed so full I couldn't see out the back window.

Two hours later I was sitting on my bed, sweating, with no idea where to put anything. My roommate walked in with one suitcase and a backpack. I looked like I was moving into a mansion. He looked like he was here for a weekend trip.

Here's the thing about move-in day. Nobody actually tells you what to expect. The college sends you a list — but that list is generic. "Twin XL sheets." "Shower caddy." Good luck.

So here's what I learned. The hard way. So you don't have to.

## It's Hot. And There's An Elevator Line.

Move-in day falls on the hottest day of the year. Every single time. You'll stand in line for the elevator with 50 other families. The stairs will look tempting. They are not. I carried a mini fridge up three flights once. Never again.

**Tip:** Bring water. Actual cold water. Not just a bottle you forgot in the car. You'll thank me by noon.

## You Will Bring Too Much

That's fine. Everyone does. But here's a filter: if you didn't use it in the first week, you probably don't need it. Pack for week one. Everything else can wait.

I used a [dorm checklist](https://dormtool.com/dorm-checklist/) to figure out what I actually needed vs what my mom thought I needed. Not the same list. Saved me a trip back to return a second lamp I absolutely did not use.

## Meet Your RA. They're Not Scary.

Your Resident Assistant is a student too. They get paid in free housing and snacks. They've seen it all. Drunk kids at 2am. Someone trying to microwave ramen without water. You'll be fine. Just say hi on day one. It makes everything easier later.

## Talk To Your Roommate Before Day One

Seriously. Send a text. Or a DM. Whatever. Figure out the basics before you're both standing in the same tiny room with your parents watching.

Who's bringing the fridge? Who's bringing the TV? Are you okay with guests? What about sleep schedules?

If you don't talk about it beforehand, you'll have an awkward conversation on week three when someone's alarm goes off at 6am and the other person wants to sleep till noon.

We made a [roommate agreement](https://dormtool.com/roommate-agreement/) before move-in day. Felt silly at first. But honestly, it saved us from like 80% of the stupid arguments I saw other roommates having.

## Parents: Let Them Help, Then Let Them Go

Your parents are going to want to stay. They'll offer to help unpack. They'll suggest furniture arrangements. Let them help for an hour. Then — gently — suggest they head out.

The real milestone of move-in day isn't when your room is set up. It's when your parents leave and you realize you're on your own for the first time. Scary. Also awesome.

## Your Room Will Not Look Like Pinterest

And that's okay. Nobody's room looks good on day one. You don't have posters up yet. Your bedding doesn't match. The lights are too bright.

Give it a week. It'll feel like home eventually.

## The First Night Is Weird

After your parents leave and you've said an awkward goodbye to your roommate who's also trying to figure out what to do — you'll probably sit on your bed and scroll your phone for an hour.

That's normal. Everyone does it. Go find your floor's common room. Someone else will be there. Say hi. Instant friends.

## A Few Quick Tips I Stole From Experience

- **Label your stuff.** Power cords, chargers, your mini fridge. Things get mixed up in shared spaces real fast.
- **Bring a pair of sandals.** Communal showers are ... not clean enough to go barefoot. Trust me.
- **A mattress topper.** The dorm mattress is a brick with fabric on it. A $30 topper changes everything.
- **Know your budget.** Move-in costs add up fast. Bedding, toiletries, snacks, eating out because the dining hall isn't open yet. Use a [rent affordability calculator](https://dormtool.com/rent-affordability/) if you're going off-campus next year. Start planning early.

## You've Got This

Move-in day is overwhelming. But it's one day. By the end of week one you'll know where your classes are, you'll have a dining hall table you call yours, and you'll wonder why you stressed so much.

Go enjoy it. And maybe leave the second lamp at home.
