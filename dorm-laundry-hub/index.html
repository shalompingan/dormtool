How to Master Your Dorm Laundry Routine

Doing laundry in a dorm is different from doing it at home. Shared machines, limited time slots, and the risk of shrinking your favorite sweater make it a skill worth learning early. This guide covers the essentials so you can keep your clothes looking good all semester.

Sorting and Pre-Treatment

Sort your laundry into three piles: whites, darks, and delicates. Check every pocket before washing — tissue and pens can ruin a full load. Treat stains immediately with a stain stick or liquid detergent; dried stains are much harder to remove. Turn jeans and dark clothes inside out to preserve color.

Using Dorm Machines

Most dorm laundry rooms have front-loading washers and dryers. Use the "cold water" setting for most loads — it saves energy, prevents shrinking, and works just as well. Set a timer on your phone. Dorm laundry rooms are shared, and leaving clothes in a machine too long is a sure way to annoy your floormates.

Drying Tips

Over-drying is the number one cause of clothing damage in dorms. Use the "low heat" or "air dry" setting for synthetic fabrics and delicates. Hang sweaters and bras to dry — the dryer will ruin their shape. A foldable drying rack in your room is a worthwhile investment for items that can't go in the dryer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I do laundry in college?

Most students do laundry once every 1-2 weeks. Plan ahead — buy enough underwear and socks to last at least 10 days so you're not scrambling. Sunday afternoons are typically the least crowded time in dorm laundry rooms.

What should I do if someone takes my clothes out of the machine?

It happens. Set a timer and be back before your cycle ends. If your clothes are moved when you return, stay calm — it's usually a floormate who needed the machine. Leave a polite note if it becomes a pattern, and talk to your RA if the issue persists.

Do I really need separate detergent for colors and whites?

Not strictly. A good quality "cold water" or "all-in-one" detergent works for both. If you want to be extra careful with whites, add a scoop of oxygen bleach (like OxiClean) to each load. Skip regular bleach — it can damage dorm machines and ruin colored clothes.

How do I get strong smells out of dorm laundry?

Dorm machines can develop mildew odors from constant use. Run a "clean cycle" with a cup of white vinegar or a washing machine cleaner tablet before your first load of the semester. For smelly laundry itself, add half a cup of baking soda to the wash cycle — it neutralizes odors without damaging fabrics.

What laundry supplies do I need for a dorm?

The basics: a laundry hamper or bag (portable enough to carry to the laundry room), detergent pods (easier than measuring liquid), dryer sheets or wool balls, a stain stick for emergencies, and a foldable drying rack for delicates. Most dorms have vending machines for detergent if you run out.
